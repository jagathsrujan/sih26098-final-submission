feasibility analysis
  • Error budget closes under the requirement — derived terms, combined in RSS (table 1)
  • Guidance margin — derived figures; the guided-window analysis re-quantifies them (table 2)
  • Mass — CAD analysis, not a weighed article; the target class is not yet met (table 2)
  • Gate: retain > 90 % of the conventional round's maximum range
  • Validation: 7-DOF Monte Carlo — 5,000 design + 5×1,000 corners, worst-corner 95 % bound — then HIL, rotating dynamometer rig (plan, not an asset), integration demonstrator
  • Standards: MIL-STD-1316 · 331C · 810F · 461 · AOP-22 Ed B Ver 1 · STANAG 4369/AOP-39 · 4439 · 9216/MIL-STD-333B
  • Manufacturing: two-tier process at 5,000 kits/yr — one moving assembly, no shell re-qualification, tooling amortised; candidate processes validated post-selection
  • Published precedent sits beside ours in table 3 — credited results; full references on slide 6
potential challenges and risks · strategies for overcoming them
  • Aerodynamic coefficient set is the acknowledged long pole → published data + CFD fill the gaps, uncertainty carried into the CEP budget
  • Mass trade → named levers; the > 90 % range-retention gate decides
  • GNSS contested → inertial coast to the drift limit; anti-jam GNSS as a module substitution; growth modules are options, never delivered capability
  • Thermal duty — 2.92 kW peak / ≈ 121 kJ per flight (derived sizing figures) → designed interface ≥ 58 W/K vs 3–16 W/K bare thread; the rig closes it
